# Captions

A publishable title per design and one description per shape (its variants share it), written as copy on what makes the shape special: shapes are named by their geometry, never by registered trade names, and nothing quotes a laboratory. The Text group fills the sheet's title and description from these.

## Round Brilliant

The round brilliant is the shape every other is measured against: fifty-seven facets worked out to return more light to the eye than any other cut, with the fire and flash that have made it the first choice for an engagement ring for a hundred years.

## Marquise Brilliant, French Tips / Marquise Brilliant, Pointed Tips / Marquise Brilliant, Side Hubs, French Tips / Marquise Brilliant, Side Hubs, Pointed Tips

Long, slender and pointed at both ends, the marquise looks larger than any other cut of the same weight and has a silhouette that is unmistakable from across a room.

## Pear Brilliant, French Tip / Pear Brilliant, Pointed Tip

A rounded head tapering to a single point, the pear brings softness and drama together in one teardrop, lit from end to end by its brilliant facets and looking larger face up than a round of the same weight.

## Heart Brilliant, French Tip / Heart Brilliant, Straighter Wings, French Tip / Heart Brilliant, Straighter Wings, Pointed Tip / Heart Brilliant, Pointed Tip

The most romantic of all the cuts and the hardest to cut well, the heart is a declaration in a single stone: two matching lobes, a crisp cleft and a clean point, full of light and impossible to mistake for anything else.

## Oval Brilliant, Eight Mains / Oval Brilliant, Mains at the Ends / Oval Brilliant, Four Mains

The oval has all the brilliance of a round with a longer, more graceful line, spreading its weight across a larger face so that it looks bigger than its carats.

## Cushion Brilliant / Cushion Brilliant, Pointed Culet

With its soft corners and pillow-shaped outline, the cushion glows rather than glitters, throwing broad warm flashes with a feel of candlelight and antique jewellery about it.

## Square Cushion Modified Brilliant

A square cushion whose pavilion has been cut into a shimmer of small facets, so the stone sparkles the way light plays on crushed ice while keeping the cushion’s gentle, pillow-shaped charm.

## Square Modified Brilliant

Crisp, square and sharp-cornered, with a pavilion of chevrons that light it from corner to corner, the square modified brilliant is the clean contemporary answer to the round and puts more of the crystal into the ring than any other brilliant.

## Cut-Cornered Rectangular Modified Brilliant

The cut-cornered rectangle takes the tailored outline of a step cut and fills it with brilliant-cut fire, dazzling from every part of the stone and hard-wearing at every corner.

## Emerald Cut

The emerald cut does not sparkle, it gleams: long open step facets turn the stone into a hall of mirrors with a cool, architectural glamour that nothing else can match.

## Square Emerald Cut

Deep clipped corners, a high crown and a small table make the square emerald cut the step cut at its most hypnotic, its facets forming a stair of concentric squares turning in to the centre with all the poise of art deco jewellery.

## Shield Brilliant, French Tip / Shield Brilliant, Pointed Tip

With a broad curved top, rounded shoulders and sides that sweep in to a point like the crest on a coat of arms, the shield is a rare, confident shape that fills its whole outline with brilliant light.

## Kite Shield, Step Cut / Hexagonal Shield, Step Cut / Wide Shield, Step Cut

Six straight sides, a pointed base and rows of flat step facets that catch the light in bold clean flashes make the step-cut shield more armour than ornament, as modern as anything cut this year.

## Triangular Brilliant, Curved Sides / Triangular Brilliant, Straight Sides / Triangular Brilliant, Corner Facets

Three sides, three points and a blaze of light: the triangular brilliant is pure geometry, with a wide flat spread that makes it look far larger than its weight.

## Single Cut

The single cut is the brilliant stripped to its essentials, eight facets above and eight below, so that even the tiniest stones keep the crisp bright flashes that have set the sparkle in pavé work for a century.

## Old Mine Cut

The old mine cut is the brilliant as it was cut by candlelight, a squarish cushion with a small table, high crown, deep pavilion and open culet that throws big soft flashes and carries the romance of antique jewellery in every facet.

## Round Preform

A round preform with no facets yet: the beginning of a design.
