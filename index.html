Drawing sheet

To add a projected view, click L, R, A or B beside a view in the list below: it projects a new view to the Left, Right, Above or Below that view under the selected standard. Projected views can be projected from again, and all of them follow the base view as you rotate it. 3D adds an isometric view of that view (choose its side in the row); drag over a 3D view to turn it on its own, shift-drag to roll it, alt-drag (option on a Mac) to move it, wheel over it to scale it, ↺ to reset it. It still follows its parent when the base turns. Click a view in this list or on the sheet to select it (the last added view is selected): the View buttons above then turn that view instead of the base, a 3D view in place, a projected view becoming a free 3D view at that orientation (↺ makes it isometric again, × removes it). With the base selected the buttons turn the base as before. lock stops a view being turned by dragging (projected views follow the base, so lock the base for those); alt-drag still moves it, and alt-drag anywhere else on the sheet moves the whole arrangement, base and projected views together, to make room for text; hidden draws that view's hidden lines in light grey. Gaps are in stone widths between view bounding boxes. Sheets are always orthographic.
Scatter stones makes a coloring page: copies of the current stone at random on the page, each in its own size (widths in millimetres on the page, never under 20 mm) and its own 3D orientation, mostly face-up and tilted between the two tilt angles towards a random side (smaller tilts more often, so most are face-up to isometric and a few nearly side-on), with the chosen share turned over to show the pavilion. Larger stones go down first; one that does not fit is tried smaller and otherwise left out. The smallest stone is the base and the rest are 3D views, so any of them can still be turned, moved (alt-drag) and scaled (wheel); hidden lines are off. The page margin is a third of an inch.
Wheel zooms about the cursor; right- or middle-button drag (or ctrl-drag) pans.
With a page set, the preview frames the page and its outline is always drawn. Stone width is the drawing scale, so it enlarges or shrinks all the projected views together; Fit views sets it so the arrangement spans the chosen share of the page and centres it. The view list's ⇔ and ⇕ buttons centre a view on the page horizontally or vertically (a 3D view moves alone; any other view moves the whole arrangement), alt-drag on the base moves the arrangement, and Export PNG writes the page at the chosen DPI (8.5 × 8.5 at 300 DPI is 2550 × 2550 pixels).
Views whose extents overlap sideways share a column, and those that overlap vertically share a row; grid lines run midway between neighbours, with the border outside the outermost views. Six views in a cross with a 3D view beside the bottom one give 3 columns by 4 rows. The grid exports with the sheet.

Text

Each shape has a publishable caption in descriptive wording (no trade names, no laboratory text); it fills the blocks until you write your own.
A title and a description drawn on the sheet and exported with it: drag either to move it, wheel over it to change its size and shift-wheel to change the width its lines wrap at (the Width field does the same), ⇔ and ⇕ centre it on the page, ↺ puts it back (the title near the top, the description under it), lock stops drags and the wheel changing it. Sizes are in points at the page scale, as they print. With the background on, the pavé stones pack right up to the letters themselves (each character is an obstacle), not just the block's rectangle.

Background

Fills the page (or a border round the sheet) with face-up stones of the current shape, right up to the views' outlines and into the spaces between them: a tangent circle packing, where every stone's circumscribed circle touches its neighbours, a view or the page edge, and gaps are filled by ever smaller stones down to the smallest. Each stone is turned at random and drawn at a random opacity between the two limits; on a round page, stones under the single-cut threshold are round single cuts, as melee is (other shapes keep their shape at every size). Sizes follow the trade weight estimators (carats = length × width × depth × shape factor), so a 1 ct stone is drawn at its real width at the page scale. For shapes the estimators cover, one stone of each chart size (0.25, 0.33, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5 ct) is drawn as a plain outline with its weight in it, at the length and width the trade size chart gives for that weight. The hexafoil pattern (marquise pages only) lays out the flower of life: circles on a triangular lattice, each lens where two circles overlap holding a marquise of one size, six meeting at every point, at one opacity, with the circles drawn as thin lines so the figure reads.

Setup
